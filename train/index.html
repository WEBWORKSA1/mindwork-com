Nine tests of reaction, memory and attention

Each test takes one to three minutes and is based on a task used in psychology research. Scores are saved in your browser, so the second run tells you more than the first.

  • Reaction timeClick when the pad turns green. Five trials, averaged in milliseconds.
  • Number memoryRemember a number that grows by one digit each round.
  • Sequence memoryRepeat a growing pattern of tiles. One slip ends the run.
  • Visual memoryTiles flash on a grid. Click the same tiles from memory.
  • Stroop testName the ink colour, ignore the word. Measures interference in milliseconds.
  • 2-backPress Match when a letter repeats the one two steps back.
  • Mental mathSixty seconds of sums. The next one appears the instant you are right.
  • Verbal memorySeen it or new? Keep words apart as the list grows.
  • Schulte tableFind the numbers 1 to 25 in order, as fast as you can.

How to use the tests

  1. Take each test once to set a baseline. First runs are usually your worst.
  2. Retest in the same conditions: same device, same time of day.
  3. Watch the trend on your dashboard, not single results. A bad night shows up here before you feel it.
  4. Use the daily workout if you want a routine: three tests, about five minutes.

These are browser versions of research tasks, scored on your own hardware. They are not IQ tests, clinical assessments or a diagnosis of anything. If you are worried about your memory or attention, talk to a doctor.

Your MindWork.com score

The dashboard converts your best result on each test to a 0 to 100 scale and averages them into one number. It is a convenience for tracking yourself over time. It is not a norm-referenced score and should not be compared with anyone else’s.
