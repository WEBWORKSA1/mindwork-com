The Pareto principle

A few causes usually produce most of the effect.

What it is

In many systems outcomes are unevenly distributed: a small share of inputs accounts for a large share of results. The 80/20 split is a rule of thumb, not a law, but the unevenness is real often enough to check for.

How to use it

  1. Pick an outcome you care about, such as revenue, bugs or complaints.
  2. List the sources and measure each one’s contribution.
  3. Sort from largest to smallest and find the small group that explains most of the total.
  4. Put effort there first.

At work

A support team tags a month of tickets. Three of forty issue types account for 61% of volume. Fixing those three does more than chipping at all forty.

Where it goes wrong

The remaining 20% of results may still matter, for example safety issues or your largest customer’s edge case.

Where it comes from

Vilfredo Pareto observed in 1896 that about 80% of Italian land was owned by 20% of the population. Joseph Juran generalised the pattern to management.

Use it today: write one decision you are facing this week and run it through the steps above. Two lines is enough.
