25 mental models for work

Compact ideas for deciding, solving and explaining. Each page gives the steps, an example from working life and the usual way the model gets misused.

  • Second-order thinkingAsk “and then what?” before you act.Decide
  • InversionWork out what would guarantee failure, then avoid it.Decide
  • Reversible and irreversible decisionsMove fast on choices you can undo; slow down on those you cannot.Decide
  • Expected valueWeigh each outcome by its probability, then add them up.Decide
  • Regret minimisationChoose what your older self would be least sorry about.Decide
  • Pre-mortemImagine the project has failed, then explain why.Decide
  • First-principles thinkingBreak the problem down to what is certainly true and build up from there.Solve
  • Five whysAsk “why?” repeatedly until you reach a cause you can fix.Solve
  • Occam’s razorPrefer the explanation that needs the fewest assumptions.Solve
  • Issue treesSplit a big question into parts that do not overlap and leave nothing out.Solve
  • The Pareto principleA few causes usually produce most of the effect.Prioritise
  • The Eisenhower matrixSort by urgent and important; they are not the same thing.Prioritise
  • Opportunity costThe real cost of a choice is the best alternative you gave up.Prioritise
  • Parkinson’s lawWork expands to fill the time available for it.Prioritise
  • The Feynman techniqueIf you cannot explain it simply, you do not understand it yet.Learn
  • Spaced repetitionReview just before you would forget, at growing intervals.Learn
  • Deliberate practicePractise the part you cannot yet do, with feedback, at full attention.Learn
  • Circle of competenceKnow where your knowledge ends, and act accordingly.Learn
  • Feedback loopsOutputs circle back and change the system that produced them.See systems
  • BottlenecksA system moves only as fast as its slowest step.See systems
  • The map is not the territoryEvery model leaves things out. Check it against reality.See systems
  • CompoundingSmall gains that build on themselves become large, slowly and then quickly.See systems
  • The pyramid principleLead with the answer, then give the reasons, then the evidence.Communicate
  • SteelmanningArgue against the strongest version of the other view, not the weakest.Communicate
  • Hanlon’s razorDo not assume bad intent when a mistake or a busy day explains it.Communicate

How to use this library

Do not try to learn all of them. Pick five that match where your work goes wrong, tie each to a situation, and use them for a month. The thinking toolkit guide has a four-week plan.
