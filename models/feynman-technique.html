The Feynman technique

If you cannot explain it simply, you do not understand it yet.

What it is

Explaining an idea in plain words, as if to someone new to the subject, exposes the gaps that familiarity hides. The gaps tell you exactly what to go back and study.

How to use it

  1. Write the concept at the top of a page.
  2. Explain it in plain language, without jargon, as if teaching a beginner.
  3. Mark every point where you got stuck or reached for a technical term.
  4. Go back to the source for those points, then simplify the explanation again.

At work

An analyst preparing to present a regression model writes a one-page explanation for the sales team. She cannot explain why one variable was dropped, rereads the method, and finds an error.

Where it goes wrong

A smooth explanation can still be wrong. Test it on a real person or against worked examples.

Where it comes from

Named after the physicist Richard Feynman, known for explaining difficult ideas in plain terms.

Use it today: write one decision you are facing this week and run it through the steps above. Two lines is enough.
