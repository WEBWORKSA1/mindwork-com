Focus timer

Work in timed focus sessions with short breaks between them. Add the tasks you plan to work on, click one to make it active, and the timer counts sessions against it.

How to use it

  1. Add the one task you will work on first.
  2. Start a 25-minute focus session and work on that task only.
  3. When the beeps sound, take the five-minute break away from the screen.
  4. After four sessions, take the long break. Change any of the lengths under the timer.

Why it works

The method is Francesco Cirillo’s Pomodoro Technique, which he developed as a student in the late 1980s using a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. The mechanics are simple, and that is the point: a short, fixed interval lowers the cost of starting, and a visible countdown makes drifting to email feel like breaking a promise.

The breaks matter as much as the sessions. In a 2011 study, Ariga and Lleras found that people who took brief breaks during a 50-minute attention task held their performance, while those who worked straight through declined. If 25 minutes feels short for deep technical work, set 50 and 10 instead; the structure matters more than the number.

Sources
  • Cirillo, F. (2006). The Pomodoro Technique.
  • Ariga, A., & Lleras, A. (2011). Brief and rare mental “breaks” keep you focused. Cognition.
