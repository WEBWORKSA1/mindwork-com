Noise mixer

Blend five steady sounds to mask a noisy room. The audio is generated on your device, so nothing downloads or streams, and your mix is remembered for next time.

How to use it

  1. Press Play sound, then raise one slider at a time.
  2. Start with brown noise around 40%. It is the least tiring for long sessions.
  3. Add a little rain or pink noise if voices still cut through.
  4. Keep the volume low enough that you could hold a conversation over it.

Why it works

Steady broadband noise works mainly by masking: it raises the floor of the sound around you, so sudden noises such as speech, doors and notifications stand out less and pull your attention less often. White noise has equal energy at every frequency and sounds like hiss; pink and brown noise carry more low-frequency energy and sound softer and deeper.

The evidence that noise makes people think better is mixed. Mehta, Zhu and Cheema found that moderate ambient noise helped on creative tasks while loud noise hurt, and Söderlund’s group found that white noise helped inattentive children while slightly hurting attentive ones. The practical rule: use noise to cover distractions, keep it quiet, and turn it off if it starts to tire you.

Sources
  • Mehta, R., Zhu, R., & Cheema, A. (2012). Is noise always bad? Exploring the effects of ambient noise on creative cognition. Journal of Consumer Research.
  • Söderlund, G., Sikström, S., & Smart, A. (2007). Listen to the noise: noise is beneficial for cognitive performance in ADHD. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry.
