How to read a study headline

  1. Find the sample. Twenty students is a hint; twenty thousand adults is evidence.
  2. Check for a comparison group. Without one, improvement may just be practice.
  3. Ask what was measured. A lab task is not the same as everyday life.
  4. Look for the word “associated”. It means the study cannot show cause.
  5. Wait for a second study. Single findings often shrink when repeated.
